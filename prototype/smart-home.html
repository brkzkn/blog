A house that quietly does the right thing

Home Assistant at the centre, Zigbee and a few Wi‑Fi devices around it, and a rule I keep coming back to: an automation that a guest has to be warned about is a bug.

Status: ongoingStarted 2022Home AssistantZigbeeVLANs
Hero photo: wall-mounted tablet in the hallway showing the home dashboard; evening light

Why

The first motivation was small: the hallway light switch is in the wrong place and always was. The second was the twins. Night feeds at 3 a.m. are easier when the kitchen lights come on at 10% instead of 100%, and when the heating knows that somebody is awake.

The deeper reason is the same one that makes me enjoy architecture work. A home is a distributed system with unreliable nodes and a very demanding user base. Getting it to feel calm is a design problem.

Principles

Local first: nothing essential depends on a cloud account. Physical switches keep working if the server is off. Every automation has a name a non-engineer would understand, and a single place to turn it off.

Lessons learned

  • 01Keep the physical switches. The best automation is one nobody needs to know exists.
  • 02Presence is a definition, not a sensor. Write down what “home” means before buying hardware.
  • 03Segment the network early. Migrating later means re-pairing everything.
  • 04Test the restore, not the backup.
